### Q1. Write a note on Principles of energy flow in ecosystems.

**Introduction:**
Energy flow is the transfer of solar energy through producers, consumers and decomposers in an ecosystem. It is unidirectional and obeys the laws of thermodynamics, making it the functional basis of ecological organization.

**Diagram / Flowchart:**

```text
Sun → Producers → Herbivores → Carnivores → Decomposers → Nutrient pool
```

**Core Answer:**

**Major Points:**
- Producers trap light through photosynthesis and convert it into chemical energy.
- Only a small fraction of incident solar radiation becomes gross primary productivity.
- Respiration losses reduce gross production to net primary productivity.
- Energy transfer across trophic levels follows Lindeman’s ten percent principle.
- Heat loss at every transfer makes food chains short and pyramids upright.
- Decomposers release nutrients, but energy itself is not recycled.

**Ecological / Evolutionary Significance:**
Energy flow links productivity, carrying capacity and ecosystem stability. A forest with high biomass stores more energy than a desert, while aquatic systems may show rapid turnover despite lower standing crop.

**Significance:**

**Conclusion:**
Thus, energy flow explains why ecosystems need continuous solar input and why trophic levels are limited. It is the central process behind productivity, food relations and ecological balance.

### Q3. Write a note on Food chain and food web.

A food chain is a linear pathway of energy transfer, while a food web is an interconnected network of many food chains. Together, they describe feeding relationships and ecological stability.

**Diagram / Flowchart:**

```text
Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Hawk
Food chains interlink → Food web
```

**Core Answer:**

**Major Points:**
- Grazing food chains begin with living green plants and herbivores.
- Detritus food chains begin with dead organic matter and decomposers.
- Food webs connect multiple chains through omnivory and alternative prey.

- Biomagnification of pollutants often follows food-chain pathways.

**Ecological / Evolutionary Significance:**
A simple chain explains direction of energy flow, but a web explains resilience. For example, if one prey declines, a predator may shift to another food source, reducing ecosystem collapse.
