# Unit 03 Topic Map: Ecology, Animal Behaviour and Evolution

## Syllabus Coverage Strategy

This unit is divided into three scoring blocks: **Ecology**, **Animal Behaviour** and **Evolution**. The question bank covers each block through concept-based, diagram-friendly and application-oriented answers.

## Ecology Angles

- Energy flow: productivity, trophic levels, thermodynamics and ecological efficiency.
- Ecological pyramids: number, biomass, energy, upright/inverted exceptions.
- Food chains and webs: grazing/detritus pathways, stability and biomagnification.
- Community organization: diversity, dominance, stratification, ecotone, succession and niche.
- Species interactions: predation, mutualism, commensalism, co-evolution and regulation.
- Ecosystem types: terrestrial, marine and freshwater ecology with adaptations.
- Ecosystem services: provisioning, regulating, cultural and supporting services.
- Pollution and climate: causes, effects, control, greenhouse effect, global warming and solutions.
- Conservation: biodiversity, hotspots of India, national parks, sanctuaries, Red Data Book and endangered species.
- Environmental governance: Kyoto Protocol, CBDR and climate mechanisms.

## Animal Behaviour Angles

- Parental care as reproductive investment and life-history strategy.
- Memory and learning as adaptive behavioural flexibility.
- Circadian rhythm as endogenous clock synchronized by zeitgebers.
- Innate behaviour through reflexes, taxis, instincts and fixed action patterns.
- Social communication through visual, chemical, acoustic and tactile signals.

## Evolution Angles

- Population dynamics: demographic equation, growth curves, carrying capacity and regulation.
- Species concepts: morphological, biological, ecological and phylogenetic concepts.
- Hardy-Weinberg law: formula, assumptions and evolutionary significance.
- Natural selection: mechanism, types, examples and neo-Darwinian linkage.
- Microevolution and macroevolution: scale, evidence, speciation and adaptive radiation.
- Evolution of horse and man: fossil sequence, adaptive trends and branching evolution.

## Exam Presentation Rules

- Start with a crisp definition and immediately draw a flowchart.
- Use ecological/evolutionary significance after factual bullets.
- Add one Indian example in conservation, pollution and ecosystem questions.
- For behaviour questions, connect mechanism with adaptive value.
- For evolution questions, use process + evidence + significance.
